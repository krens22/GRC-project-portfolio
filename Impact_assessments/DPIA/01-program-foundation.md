## Section 1: Program Foundation

### 1.1 Purpose

Brightwell Home relies on outside companies (vendors) to run payroll, host its website, process payments, and support customers. Each vendor that touches our data or systems creates risk we do not fully control. This program defines how Brightwell identifies its vendors, ranks them by risk, and reviews them in proportion to that risk, so that our review effort goes where a failure would hurt most.

### 1.2 Scope

This program applies to any third party that:

- stores, processes, or can access Brightwell customer or employee personal data,
- connects to Brightwell systems or networks, or
- supports a function the business cannot operate without for more than a short period.

It covers data subject to GDPR (EU customers and staff), CCPA/CPRA (California customers), and PIPEDA (Canadian customers). One-time purchases with no data or system access, such as office furniture, are out of scope.

### 1.3 Roles and Responsibilities

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| GRC Team (Third-Party Risk Analyst) | Maintains the vendor inventory, assigns tiers, sends questionnaires, rates findings, tracks remediation |
| Business Owner | The employee who requested the vendor. Confirms what data and access the vendor actually has and helps get questionnaire answers |
| Privacy Lead | Decides whether a vendor requires a Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) |
| Vendor | Completes questionnaires accurately and fixes agreed findings within the deadline |

### 1.4 Vendor Tiering Criteria

Each vendor is placed in a tier based on three questions:

1. **Data sensitivity:** How sensitive is the data the vendor can access?
2. **Business impact:** How badly would Brightwell be affected if the vendor failed or was unavailable?
3. **System access:** Does the vendor connect directly to Brightwell systems?

The highest-risk answer decides the tier.

| Tier | Criteria | Review depth | Frequency |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tier 1: Critical** | Handles highly sensitive data (SINs, bank details, payment data, or all company data), or an outage would stop sales or payroll | SIG Lite or CSA CAIQ, plus review of a SOC 2 or ISO 27001 report | Annual |
| **Tier 2: High** | Handles personal data of lower sensitivity, or an outage is disruptive but a workaround exists | SIG Lite | Annual |
| **Tier 3: Medium** | Handles only limited or pseudonymized data, with no direct system access | Short 10-question custom form | Every 2 years |
| **Tier 4: Low** | No access to data or systems | Basic check at onboarding (company legitimacy, no known breaches) | At onboarding only |

### 1.5 DPIA Triggers

A DPIA is required when a vendor processes personal data in a way likely to create high risk for individuals. Typical triggers are:

- large-scale monitoring or evaluation of people (for example, employee performance scoring),
- automated decisions that significantly affect people,
- transfers of personal data outside the EU, or
- processing of sensitive categories of data.

The Privacy Lead makes the final decision, and the reasoning is recorded in the vendor inventory.

### 1.6 Reassessment Triggers

Vendors are reviewed on the schedule in 1.4. A vendor is also reassessed immediately if it:

- reports a security breach,
- changes what data it handles or where the data is stored,
- changes subcontractors that handle Brightwell data, or
- is acquired by another company.
