## Plain-English Explanations

| # | Control | In plain English |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Access control | Each person can only open the doors they need for their job, like a hotel key card that only works on your floor. |
| 2 | Multi-factor authentication (MFA) | Logging in takes two proofs of identity, such as a password plus a code on your phone, like needing both a card and a PIN at an ATM. |
| 3 | Encryption | Data is scrambled so it's unreadable without the right key, like sending a letter in a locked box instead of a postcard. |
| 4 | Logging and monitoring | The company keeps a record of who did what on its systems and watches for anything unusual, like security cameras plus someone checking the footage. |
| 5 | Incident response | There is a written plan for what to do when something goes wrong, like a fire drill that everyone has practiced. |
| 6 | Vendor management | Companies that handle your data are checked and held to your security standards, like making sure a moving company is trustworthy before it carries your belongings. |
| 7 | Backup and recovery | Copies of important data are stored safely and tested, so lost data can be restored, like keeping a spare house key somewhere safe and checking that it works. |
| 8 | Change management | Changes to systems are approved, tested, and written down before they go live, like an inspector signing off before a building renovation. |
| 9 | Vulnerability management | Weak spots in systems are regularly searched for and fixed, like checking your home for unlocked windows and a broken lock. |
| 10 | Security awareness training | Staff learn to spot threats like phishing emails, since people are often the easiest way in, like teaching everyone in a building not to hold the door for strangers. |
