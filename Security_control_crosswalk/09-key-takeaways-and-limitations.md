## Key Takeaways

- **One control can serve several audits.** All 10 controls in this crosswalk map to at least three of the four frameworks, so building each control once and reusing the evidence saves effort.
- **Start with the controls that count the most.** Encryption, incident response, and vendor management are rated Direct in all four frameworks, which makes them high-value starting points.
- **SOC 2 and ISO 27001 are the closest fit for a control-based approach.** All 10 controls are rated Direct in both, because both frameworks are built around controls an organization chooses and runs.
- **GDPR needs the other frameworks to fill in the detail.** It names only four of the ten controls directly, and for the rest it asks for "appropriate" measures without saying what they are.
- **Frameworks leave different gaps.** PCI DSS has no general backup requirement, while GDPR names the ability to restore data explicitly, so following one framework alone can leave a gap in another.
- **Controls are only part of compliance.** ISO 27001 also requires a risk assessment and management system, and GDPR also requires privacy measures such as lawful basis and individuals' rights, none of which this crosswalk covers.

## Limitations and Disclaimer

This crosswalk is a learning project. It is not audit, legal, or compliance advice.

- **Mappings are interpretations.** They reflect the author's reading of the official framework text. Other reviewers may rate some links differently, particularly for GDPR, where requirements are broad.
- **Versions matter.** The mappings use SOC 2 (2017 Trust Services Criteria), ISO/IEC 27001:2022, PCI DSS v4.0.1, and GDPR (Regulation (EU) 2016/679). Requirement numbers may change in later versions.
- **Scope is limited.** The crosswalk covers 10 controls, the SOC 2 Security category plus Availability criteria A1.2 and A1.3, and the security aspects of GDPR. It does not map every requirement in each framework.
- **A Direct rating is not a pass.** It means a control addresses a requirement. Whether an organization passes an audit depends on how the control is implemented, its scope, the evidence provided, and the auditor's judgment.
- **Evidence examples are illustrative.** Real auditors set their own evidence requests.
- **Readers should verify against the official texts** and consult a qualified auditor or legal counsel before relying on any mapping.
