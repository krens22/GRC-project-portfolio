## Company Context

The examples in this crosswalk, especially the evidence examples, are based on a fictional company so the controls read as real work. The company is illustrative only. It does not represent a real organization.

### About the company

**Northbridge Analytics** is a fictional software-as-a-service (SaaS) company that provides analytics dashboards to business customers. Its product is hosted in the cloud and used by customers in North America and the European Union.

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| Business | Subscription analytics platform for business customers |
| Size | About 150 employees |
| Hosting | Cloud-hosted infrastructure |
| Data held | Customer account details (names, work email addresses) and product usage data, which count as personal data |
| Payments | Customers pay for subscriptions by credit card, handled through a third-party payment processor |
| Vendors | Uses outside providers for cloud hosting, payment processing, customer support, and email |

### Why each framework applies

| Framework | Why it applies to Northbridge |
|---|---|
| SOC 2 | Business customers ask for a SOC 2 report during their vendor reviews before signing a contract |
| ISO 27001 | Northbridge wants an internationally recognized certification to show customers that its security program is managed |
| PCI DSS | Northbridge accepts card payments, so it must protect cardholder data or confirm that its payment processor does |
| GDPR | Northbridge holds personal data of people in the European Union, so it must keep that data secure |

### How this context is used

The company profile is used only to make the examples concrete, mainly in the evidence examples (Section 8). The control mappings in Section 5 are not specific to Northbridge and apply to any organization that faces these four frameworks.
